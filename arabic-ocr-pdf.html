Right to left, in reading order

The difficult part of Arabic OCR is often not the letters but the order. A line of Arabic runs right to left, while the numbers, percentages and Latin words inside it run left to right. If the order is wrong, copied text comes out scrambled and searches miss.

BixoPDF puts right-to-left lines back in reading order, with numbers, percentages, brackets and punctuation where they belong. Pages set in two columns are read column by column, so the text follows the order you read in.

How it works

  1. Open the scan. Open the PDF in BixoPDF. For faint photocopies, adjust thickness, sharpness, contrast or brightness first, with a live preview. Tilted pages are straightened before reading.
  2. Run OCR. Start recognition from the OCR panel, with Arabic as the language. It runs in the background: open other PDFs in their own tabs and keep working while a long document is read. Batch OCR takes a whole folder of PDFs.
  3. Check and save. Every recognized line is boxed on the page and listed beside it. When you save, the text goes into the PDF as an invisible layer under the page image. The page looks as it did, but search, selection and copy now work in BixoPDF and in any other PDF reader.

Text that an older program hid under the scan is replaced with the new result. You can export the OCR text as plain text, or save the PDF as a Word document or Excel workbook with the OCR text included. Compressing the file afterwards keeps the text layer, so a smaller copy is still searchable.

OCR uses a lot of memory. On a PC with limited memory, save your work and let one OCR job finish before starting the next.

Your documents stay on your PC

The recognizers are installed with the app and run on your computer. Contracts, court papers, personal records and unpublished manuscripts do not have to be sent to an online service to become searchable. OCR works with no internet connection and no account.

An app that also works in Arabic

BixoPDF's own menus and windows are available in Arabic, laid out right to left, and can be switched under Settings → Language. Watermarks in Arabic are set as Windows shows them. OCR reads 50 languages whatever the app's language, so a page with Arabic and English can be handled in the same app.

To be clear about the limits: we have not yet published an accuracy measurement for Arabic, and Arabic is not among the seven languages of the built-in dictionary. The trial is the best way to see how BixoPDF handles your own documents.
